Create a 30-second ultra-realistic personal home video set in South Korea in 2004. It should look genuinely recorded on a consumer MiniDV camcorder of that era. The camera itself is NEVER visible. Natural handheld movement, imperfect autofocus, slight exposure changes, authentic DV texture and colors, ordinary real-life framing. No cinematic shots or modern-looking visuals.

A Korean woman in her early 20s excitedly shows her brand-new silver flip phone to her female friend outside a small neighborhood convenience store. They laugh and examine it.

Girl: “나 핸드폰 생겼어!”
Friend: “진짜? 보여줘!”

The girl opens and closes the phone, takes a blurry photo of her friend, and proudly shows it to her. They laugh.

They walk together through a quiet Korean residential neighborhood. Old cars, small shops, apartment buildings, bicycles and pedestrians in the background. The girl keeps checking her new phone.

At a street corner, they say goodbye.

Friend: “집에 도착하면 전화해.”
Girl: “알았어.”

They walk in opposite directions.

The girl continues home while casually filming her surroundings. A few seconds later, her new phone suddenly rings. She looks excited and answers.

“여보세요?”

She listens. Her smile slowly disappears.

She looks across the street.

Her friend is standing there, completely still, holding a phone to her ear and looking directly at her.

The girl looks down at her own phone. The call is still connected.

She slowly looks back across the street.

Her friend is now gone.

The girl remains frozen and confused as the MiniDV recording continues for a few seconds.

Cut to black.

Keep the entire video feeling like a genuine forgotten personal recording from 2004, with natural Korean dialogue and no obvious AI-generated look.
