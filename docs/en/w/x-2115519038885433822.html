The Priest on the Pier

A single continuous dolly zoom keeps a priest the same size at the end of a stormy pier while the church and coast recede behind him. Hitchcock's vertigo effect used to externalize isolation.

Prompt :

[Global Setting] 30-second single continuous shot, 16:9, 4K, photoreal, psychological drama. A grey stormy bay at dusk. A half-submerged wooden pier stretching out into the water. Across the bay, a small stone church on the coastline. No text on screen.

[Character] A priest in his 50s, black cassock and long black coat whipping in the wind, white collar, grey hair. Standing still at the very end of the pier, facing the camera.

[0–6s] Medium shot. The priest stands at the end of the pier, coat flapping. Across the water, the church bell rings once, then fades into silence.

[6–20s] Slow dolly zoom: the camera physically moves toward him while zooming out, so he stays exactly the same size in frame while the background stretches away behind him. The pier lengthens, the coastline recedes and shrinks into the distance. Water slowly rises around the wooden posts.

[20–26s] Realization hits his face: eyes widening, breath catching. Behind him, the coastline and the church fade into the grey horizon until they disappear completely.

[26–30s] Only the priest remains, alone on a pier surrounded by endless water. Hold.

[Camera] One continuous dolly zoom: camera moves in, lens zooms out, subject size locked. Slow, unhurried, queasy. No cuts.

[Audio] Wind, waves against the posts, one distant church bell, then the sound thins out into a high, steady ringing tone.

[Consistency] Same priest, same framing size throughout. Only the background changes.
