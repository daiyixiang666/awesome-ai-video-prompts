A Japanese Heian-period picture scroll (emaki) painting, horizontal 3:2, in the spirit of a classic seek-and-find book: a grand aristocratic estate in full festival, seen from an extremely high and wide oblique bird's-eye view with the roofs lifted off so that you look down into the halls, so that everything is tiny and there is an enormous amount to look at. In the middle a south courtyard of white gravel with a raised red-railed dance platform where court dancers in red and green robes perform, with court musicians playing lutes, zithers, flutes and drums; long covered corridors and wooden verandas with hanging bamboo blinds and folding screens painted with landscapes; a large garden pond with islets, pine trees, an arched red bridge and a winding garden stream with irises; plain wooden pleasure boats (square-ended, no animal-head prows) with poets and ladies; courtiers kicking a ball among cherry blossoms and maples; a gatehouse with ox-carriages parked WITHOUT oxen; kitchens with cooks and servants carrying lacquer trays; children, guards, monks. Horizontal bands of flat gold-leaf mist with fine gold-dust flecks drift between the scenes, but never cover a person or a cat. About 180 minuscule people, each doing something. A standing person is only about 3% of the picture height.

The look is the point: the Heian "tsukuri-e" picture-scroll manner (as in the Tale of Genji scrolls): opaque mineral pigments (cinnabar red, malachite green, azurite blue, ochre, lead white) laid flat over a white ground, thin brown-black ink outlines, "line for an eye, hook for a nose" faces, long straight black hair and layered many-coloured robes on the ladies, black caps and stiff court robes on the men, fine craquelure in the pigment, aged silk-paper texture. Not a photograph, not 3D, not a modern illustration. The drawing fills the whole picture to all four edges: no frame, no border, no title strip, no seals.

Exactly one small logo: a small black lacquered board hanging from the eaves of a small gatehouse in the right half, about 4% of the picture width, with a white paw print whose big pad holds a swirling ocean wave, and the word "Shoji" in white cursive brush lettering to its right. Apart from that board there are no letters, words, numbers, kanji or kana anywhere: no scroll text, no captions, no seals; poem papers and letters are blank.

THE ONLY ANIMALS IN THE WHOLE PICTURE ARE FOUR CATS, and there are never more than four. Not five, not a spare one. No other animal at all: no oxen, horses, birds (no cranes or phoenixes), fish or carp, turtles, butterflies, dogs, dragons, lion-dogs, no animal masks, no animal-head boat prows, and no animals on screens, robes or ornaments. No person wears an animal head or costume. The four cats are tiny, all in the upper and middle part of the picture: their feet are between about 15% and 60% of the picture height measured from the top, and NONE is in the lower third (the lower third is only pond, boats, stream, irises, gravel, gold mist and plants). Each cat is in the open, never hidden behind anything. The three real cats are each only about 1.5% of the picture height (about 20 pixels in a 1360-pixel-high picture), under half the height of a standing person, and wear a thin red collar with a tiny gold bell:
1. a black-and-white "hachiware" cat (black head and back, a white blaze splitting the face, white chest and paws) sitting upright on the wooden veranda of the left-hand hall beside a red lacquer box, in the left half;
2. a brown mackerel tabby (warm brown fur with dark stripes, white muzzle and chest) sitting upright on a flat garden stone at the foot of a pine tree on the far bank of the pond near the red arched bridge, upper centre-left;
3. a calico cat (white with orange and black patches) sitting on the wooden steps of the main hall below a bamboo blind, among a row of sandals, right of centre;
4. Shoji Cat, an upright orange tabby cat about three heads tall in a black leather jacket, denim shorts, cream sneakers and black wayfarer sunglasses with blue and red lenses, dancing with one arm raised on the dance platform among the court dancers, in the middle of the picture, about one and a half times as tall as each of the other three cats.
No dark round objects, no piles of black hair or cloth, and no dark animal-like shapes that could be mistaken for a cat. No watermark.
